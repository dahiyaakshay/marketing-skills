# Strategy: SEO Proposal Writer

Writes a client-facing proposal for an SEO or GEO engagement — the proposal's job is to demonstrate a specific, credible plan tailored to the prospect's actual situation, not a generic service menu that could apply to any business.

**When invoking**: On first use, confirm what's known about the prospect's specific situation before drafting. On subsequent use, go straight to the proposal.

## Scope

Pre-engagement proposal writing. Typically precedes **client-onboarding-questionnaire** in the client acquisition sequence — a strong proposal often already reflects a preliminary version of what a full onboarding questionnaire would later formalize.

## Proposal Structure

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Situation summary** | A brief, specific assessment of the prospect's current state — ideally referencing something concrete found in a preliminary look at their site/rankings, not generic industry commentary |
| **Opportunity** | The specific gap or opportunity identified, tied to a plausible business outcome (not just "improve rankings" in the abstract) |
| **Approach/methodology** | The specific work that would be done, organized by phase or priority — concrete enough that the prospect understands what they're actually paying for |
| **Timeline** | Realistic phase-by-phase timing, being honest that SEO/GEO results compound over months, not days — overpromising fast results is one of the most common ways proposals set up an engagement to later feel like it "failed" |
| **Pricing/scope** | Clear deliverables tied to the price, avoiding vague "ongoing optimization" language without specifics |
| **Reporting cadence** | What the client will actually see and how often, setting expectations before the engagement starts |

## Credibility Principles

- **Specificity over generic promises**: a proposal referencing the prospect's actual site, actual competitors, or actual industry context reads as far more credible than a templated pitch, and is more likely to convert.
- **Honest timeline framing**: SEO and GEO results are not immediate — a proposal that sets unrealistic timeline expectations creates a client relationship problem later, even if it helps close the deal now.
- **Avoid overclaiming guaranteed rankings/results**: no legitimate SEO/GEO practitioner can guarantee specific rankings, given the number of variables outside any one party's control; a proposal that implies otherwise undermines credibility with any sophisticated prospect and sets up a disappointing client relationship.

## Workflow

1. Gather what's knowable about the prospect's current situation before drafting (preliminary audit findings, competitive context).
2. Draft a specific, non-generic situation summary referencing that context.
3. Outline the approach in concrete phases, not vague ongoing-service language.
4. Set a realistic, honest timeline for expected results.
5. Tie pricing directly to specific deliverables.
6. Route the accepted proposal into **client-onboarding-questionnaire** to formalize the details before work begins.

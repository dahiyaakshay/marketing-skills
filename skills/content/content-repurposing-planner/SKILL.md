# Content: Content Repurposing Planner

Plans how a single source piece of content should be adapted across formats and channels, matched to what each format/channel actually does well rather than mechanically reformatting the same material everywhere.

**When invoking**: On first use, briefly note that repurposing should adapt to the destination format, not just copy-paste. On subsequent use, go straight to the plan.

## Scope

Cross-format/channel adaptation planning for a specific source piece. Not identifying which content is worth repurposing in the first place (see **content-scoring-rubric**, **evergreen-content-identifier**).

## Format Mapping

| Source | Destination format | Adaptation needed |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form blog post / whitepaper | Social posts | Extract single, self-contained claims or stats — one idea per post, not a summary of the whole piece |
| Long-form guide | Email newsletter | Reframe around one actionable takeaway with a link to the full piece, not a full re-summary |
| Data-heavy report | Infographic / chart | Isolate the 2–3 most visually compelling data points rather than trying to represent every finding |
| Written guide | Video/short-form script | Restructure around a spoken narrative arc — written structure (headings, bullets) doesn't translate directly to spoken delivery |
| Case study | Sales/outreach material | Lead with the outcome and quantified result, not the narrative build-up appropriate for a published case study |

## Selection Criteria for Repurposing

Only strong source material is worth the repurposing investment — content that scored well on **content-scoring-rubric** and/or is evergreen (see **evergreen-content-identifier**) has a longer shelf life across every derived format, whereas repurposing a time-sensitive or thin piece just multiplies weak material across more channels.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the source piece is strong enough to warrant repurposing (see selection criteria above).
2. Identify target formats/channels based on where the audience actually engages, not every format available.
3. For each target format, adapt — not copy — the content to match how that format is actually consumed.
4. Sequence the repurposed pieces relative to the source's publication (some channels work better as a lead-in, others as a follow-up).
5. Track which repurposed format drove the most engagement or traffic back to the source, to refine future repurposing choices.

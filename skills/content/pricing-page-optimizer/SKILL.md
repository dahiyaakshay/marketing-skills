# Content: Pricing Page Optimizer

Structures pricing-tier copy to reduce decision friction and clearly guide the reader toward the tier that fits them — a pricing page's job is to make the choice easy, not just to disclose numbers.

**When invoking**: On first use, confirm the tier structure and any recommended/highlighted tier. On subsequent use, go straight to drafting.

## Scope

Pricing-page-specific copy and structure. Not general product page content (see **product-page-optimizer**).

## Structural Elements

| Element | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Tier names** | Descriptive of the buyer, not just arbitrary labels ("Starter," "Growth," "Enterprise" over "Plan A/B/C") |
| **Recommended tier highlight** | One tier visually emphasized as the default recommendation, reducing decision paralysis |
| **Feature comparison** | Table format, consistent row order across tiers so differences are scannable at a glance |
| **FAQ** | Addresses common pricing objections directly (billing cycle, refunds, what happens at tier limits) |
| **Social proof near price** | Trust signals close to the point of decision, not only elsewhere on the site |

## Reducing Decision Friction

Too many tiers, or tiers with unclear differentiation, increase cognitive load and can suppress conversion — if tier boundaries aren't obviously meaningful to the buyer, consider consolidating. Highlighting one tier as the recommended default gives undecided buyers a low-effort default choice rather than forcing them to evaluate every option from scratch.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the tier structure and whether a specific tier should be highlighted as recommended.
2. Name tiers around buyer type/need rather than arbitrary labels.
3. Build the feature comparison table with consistent row structure.
4. Draft an FAQ addressing common pricing objections.
5. Place trust signals near the pricing decision point, not only elsewhere on the page.

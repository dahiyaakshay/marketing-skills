# Off-Page: Broken Link Building

Finds broken (404) outbound links on relevant third-party pages and pitches the site owner a working replacement link to equivalent content on the target site — a link building method that offers genuine value (fixing something broken on their site) rather than just requesting a favor.

**When invoking**: On first use, briefly note the genuine-value framing below. On subsequent use, go straight to prospecting.

## Scope

Broken-link-specific prospecting and outreach. For broader prospecting methods, see **link-prospecting**.

## Method

| Step | What it produces |
|---|---|
| Find resource/link pages relevant to the target site's topic | Candidate pages likely to contain outbound links worth checking |
| Check outbound links on those pages for 404s | The actual broken-link opportunities |
| Confirm the target site has content that's a genuine, close replacement | Not just any content — content that would actually satisfy the original linking purpose |
| Pitch the site owner, noting the broken link specifically | The core outreach message |

## Why This Method Converts Well

Unlike a cold guest post pitch, a broken link report gives the site owner something they'd want regardless of whether they link back — a working page is better for their own visitors and their own site's user experience. Leading with "I noticed this link on your page is broken" (verified genuine, not a pretext) and only then suggesting a specific replacement tends to have a notably higher response rate than link requests framed purely as a favor.

## Workflow

1. Identify relevant resource/link pages in the target site's topic area.
2. Check outbound links on those pages for broken (404) destinations.
3. Confirm the target site has genuinely relevant, high-quality content that would serve as a good replacement.
4. Draft outreach leading with the specific broken link found, then suggest the replacement as a helpful fix.
5. Follow up once if no response, but don't pursue aggressively — the value proposition should carry the pitch.

# Off-Page: Digital PR Pitch Writer

Pitches proactive, outbound story ideas to journalists — original data, a newsworthy angle, or a timely expert commentary opportunity — distinct from responding to an inbound journalist query (see **haro-response-writer**).

**When invoking**: On first use, confirm there's a genuinely newsworthy angle before drafting. On subsequent use, go straight to the pitch.

## Scope

Proactive outbound media pitching. For inbound query responses, see **haro-response-writer**. For the underlying press release document, see **press-release-writer** (content category).

## What Makes a Pitch Newsworthy

A pitch built around original data, a genuinely surprising finding, or a timely connection to current events tends to succeed; a pitch that's essentially a product announcement dressed up as news tends to be ignored by journalists, who receive high volumes of exactly that kind of pitch daily. The strongest pitches answer "why would a reader outside our own customer base care about this" before anything else.

## Structural Elements

| Element | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Subject line** | States the newsworthy hook directly, not a vague teaser |
| **Opening line** | The core finding or angle, in one sentence — journalists decide whether to keep reading within seconds |
| **Supporting data/evidence** | Specific, credible numbers or examples backing the angle |
| **Relevance to this specific journalist/publication** | Reference their beat or recent coverage showing this pitch was targeted, not blasted to a generic list |
| **Easy next step** | Offer availability for a quick call or additional data, not a demand for a specific placement |

## Workflow

1. Confirm the pitch has a genuine hook beyond a straightforward company announcement.
2. Identify journalists/publications whose beat genuinely matches the story.
3. Draft a subject line and opening line that lead with the hook, not the company name.
4. Include specific supporting data or examples.
5. Personalize each pitch to the specific journalist's recent coverage or beat.
6. Send with an easy, low-friction next step rather than a hard ask.

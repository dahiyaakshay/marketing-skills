# Off-Page: Guest Post Outreach

Drafts outreach emails pitching a guest post placement — the pitch's job is to demonstrate genuine value to the target site's audience and editorial standards, not just request a link.

**When invoking**: On first use, confirm the target site and a specific angle before drafting. On subsequent use, go straight to the pitch.

## Scope

Outreach messaging for guest post placement. For identifying which sites to pitch, see **link-prospecting** or **competitor-backlink-gap**.

## Structural Elements

| Element | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Personalization** | Reference something specific about the target site (a recent post, their specific audience/angle) — generic mass-outreach templates are easily identified and typically ignored |
| **Clear, specific pitch** | 2–3 concrete topic ideas tailored to that site's audience, not a vague "I'd like to write for you" |
| **Credibility signal** | A brief, relevant credential or link to a comparable published piece, kept short |
| **Low-friction ask** | Make it easy to say yes — offer to adapt the pitch based on their feedback rather than presenting it as take-it-or-leave-it |
| **No overt link demand upfront** | Focus the pitch on content value; link placement is a natural, expected part of a guest post, not something to negotiate aggressively in the first message |

## Why Personalization Matters More Than Volume

A small number of genuinely personalized pitches to well-matched sites converts at a meaningfully higher rate than a large volume of generic template outreach — editors and site owners can quickly identify templated mass outreach, and it tends to damage the sender's reputation with that site going forward, closing off future opportunities.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the target site fits the site's actual audience and content quality bar.
2. Research something specific and recent about the target site to reference in the opener.
3. Draft 2–3 concrete, tailored topic pitches rather than a vague general offer.
4. Keep the credibility signal brief and relevant.
5. End with a low-friction next step, not an aggressive ask.
6. Follow up once, politely, if no response — avoid repeated unsolicited follow-ups.
